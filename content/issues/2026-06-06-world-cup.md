## Anthropic Expands Mythos Access

So Anthropic dropped some news on June 2nd: they're expanding access to Mythos, their vulnerability-hunting AI tool, four-fold, which brings the total to around 200 Glasswing partners. If you haven't been following along, the basic idea is that you point Mythos at a big pile of code and it goes hunting for the security holes nobody noticed were there, the kind of bugs that usually take a skilled human days or weeks to dig up ([or sometimes ones that were never found](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-mythos-cybersecurity-zero-day-vulnerabilities)). That's genuinely useful, and you can see why the demand is what it is. Governments and big financial institutions are reportedly lining up around the block, and the EU's cybersecurity agency is going to be the first EU body to get its hands on it.

What makes this one stand out to me is who's at the front of the line. It's not a bunch of startups looking for an edge, it's national governments and the institutions that quietly hold a frightening amount of the world's money. When that's your customer base, you're not really selling a developer tool anymore, you're selling a piece of national security infrastructure, and that changes the whole conversation around it. Anthropic clearly knows that, which is probably why the rollout has been this slow drip of partners instead of just flipping it on for everybody at once.

Here's where I start to squint a little, though. A tool that's really, really good at finding vulnerabilities is, well, really good at finding vulnerabilities. It doesn't actually care whether you're trying to patch the hole or crawl through it. The same model that helps the EU lock down its critical infrastructure is the model somebody else would love to point at that exact same infrastructure, and a bunch of officials have started saying as much, which I think is fair. I'm not in the "shut it all down" camp, but four-fold is a big jump, and 200 partners is a lot of doors to keep track of. All it takes is one of them getting sloppy with their access, or one bad hire, and suddenly the most powerful bug-finder on the planet is quietly working for the wrong team. So who exactly makes the cut for those 200? That's the part I'd actually want to read about, and the part nobody seems too eager to spell out.

## United States SPR Inventory Slides

While the rest of us are busy arguing about World Cup brackets, something a lot less fun has been happening quietly in the background. The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve, which is basically the giant emergency stash of oil we're supposed to be able to lean on when things go sideways, has lost more than 50 million barrels since the war in Iran kicked off, and it's now creeping toward an all-time low. Remember when Trump promised he'd fill the thing back up "to the top"? Yeah, that one didn't exactly age well.

The part that actually worries me is the timing. We're walking straight into the summer driving season, when everybody and their mother is out on the road, and that's the worst possible moment to open the cupboard and find it getting bare. Commercial inventories are thinning out too, so it's not like there's some big private buffer sitting around to pick up the slack. And this isn't only an American problem. Vitol's top guy in the Middle East came right out and said Western governments aren't taking the global supply crunch seriously, with the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz pinching off a huge chunk of the oil that's supposed to be reaching everyone else. When you remember that something like a fifth of the world's oil normally moves through that one skinny little strait, that's not a small thing to shrug off.

And this is what really nags at me about the whole setup. The entire point of a strategic reserve is to have it sitting there full before the emergency, not to go scraping the bottom of the barrel once one's already underway. If something actually escalates over in the Gulf, and given everything going on lately that's not some wild hypothetical, you'd want that cushion fat and ready, not run down to historic lows. Refilling it isn't as simple as flipping a switch, either. You have to go out and buy a mountain of oil on the open market, which tends to shove the price up at exactly the moment you'd rather it didn't, so there's a real chicken-and-egg problem with ever getting it back to where it should be.

Look, I'm not trying to send anyone into a panic about five-dollar gas. But when the people who literally trade and ship this stuff for a living are talking this bluntly, I tend to figure it's worth a look. This is the story I'll keep half an eye on all summer, even if it never really cracks the headlines while there's a tournament on.

## Ebola Worsens In The DRC

This is the one on the list that genuinely keeps me up a bit. In the span of just a few days, the Ebola outbreak in the DRC jumped from 13 affected health zones to 22, spread across three different provinces in the east, and the officials on the ground are still scrambling just to work out how big this thing actually is. That last part is what gets me. When the number of places reporting cases is climbing faster than anyone can keep track of, it usually means you're already a step or two behind the virus, and with something like Ebola you really, really do not want to be behind.

It also doesn't help that all of this is happening in eastern Congo, a region that's been dealing with serious conflict for years. From what I understand, that turns the basic work of stopping an outbreak (finding who's been exposed, reaching patients while there's still time, keeping clinics open and staffed) into something far harder than it would be almost anywhere else. You end up fighting the disease and the chaos around it at the same time, and neither one waits for the other.

It's also worth remembering what we're actually talking about here. Ebola isn't some abstract line on a chart, it's one of the most frightening diseases on the planet, with fatality rates that can run terribly high when people can't get treatment in time. Every one of those 22 health zones is full of families, health workers, and whole communities trying to go about their lives while something genuinely terrifying spreads around them. It's easy to file a headline like this one under "far away, not my problem," especially when there's a tournament to watch, but the people actually living through it don't get that luxury.

Then there's the line that made messed with me: Ebola has somehow entered the conversation in Brazil. I'm not going to pretend I know what that means yet, because as far as I can tell, nobody really does. It might genuinely be nothing. But if you think back to the 2014 outbreak in West Africa, the truly scary moments were always the ones where the virus turned up somewhere new and far away, somewhere it had no business being. So for now I'm just going to keep reading, keep hoping it stays boxed in where it is, and root for the most boring possible update next time around. Some weeks, boring is the best news you can ask for.

## World Cup 2026: United States, Mexico, & Canada

Well, well, well. After all the noise and uproar about ticket prices, transportation fares, and civil unrest, we find ourselves awaiting the largest sporting event on Earth. The tournament has now expanded to 48 teams, which means there are a lot more games (and a lot more money for FIFA). Given that I like to make predictions (and I like to be right about things), I'm going to use a model I built to help simulate the tournament and aid my predictions for this year's World Cup. I tried doing this back in 2022 without a model, and it went pretty horrendously (I think I had Brazil taking the title), so here's to another shot! The approach involves conducting a Monte Carlo simulation with about 10,000 trials. I employed the Elo rating system to analyze team performance and complemented it with a Poisson Distribution for goal prediction. This approach enhances our understanding of the match dynamics. The likelihood of various scores between two teams allows us to make predictions on any single game. A YouTuber, [Right of the Bell](https://www.youtube.com/@rightofthebell), explains this way better than I and in more detail in [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w5NK7bPjQkw), so go check that out to learn more about the predictive process. I'm not going to explain the full breakdown of the tournament and how FIFA decided to make it (it's your job to figure that one out yourself), but I'll quickly go through my predictions at each stage (Group Stage, 3rd-place teams advancing, Round of 32, Round of 16, etc.).
